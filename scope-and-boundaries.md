# Scope and boundaries

This page states where my evidence is strongest and where it stops. It exists so a hiring team can calibrate quickly rather than discovering the edges in an interview.

## Where my evidence is strongest

My strongest evidence is in enterprise technical content, product adoption, customer education, documentation systems, and AI-assisted content operations.

Specifically: owning large content sets for complex cloud products, auditing and reorganizing information architecture, writing for technical buyers and operators, verifying technical claims against authoritative sources, building and operating AI-assisted production workflows with quality gates and human review, evaluating content retrievability for AI answering systems, and orchestrating contributions from product managers, engineers, and field teams.

## What requires separate evidence

These are capabilities the role market asks about that my record does not establish. I am marking them so nobody has to guess.

**Demand generation and pipeline attribution.** I have not owned marketing campaigns, and I cannot show content attributed to pipeline or revenue. My measurement history is documentation telemetry, customer satisfaction, freshness, page views, and learner completion rates.

**Marketing programs.** I have delivered live instructor-led technical training, software demonstrations, and instructional video, but I have not run a recurring webinar program, produced named customer stories or industry research reports, written under an executive byline, built account-based programs, owned social channels, or used marketing analytics tools such as Google Analytics 4, Search Console, Ahrefs, or Semrush.

**Formal people management.** My leadership evidence is program leadership, coordination and editing of vendor and outsourced writers, standards enforcement, and backlog ownership rather than a direct-report management history.

## Technical boundaries stated precisely

**Kubernetes.** I created and configured Azure Kubernetes Service clusters to validate the monitoring procedures I documented, and I owned Azure Monitor documentation concerning Azure Kubernetes Service for roughly six months. I also wrote and tested Prometheus, Grafana, and OpenTelemetry guidance in my own Azure lab, and I partnered with the product manager on features in progress. My depth is documentation and validation, not cluster engineering, so I do not claim cluster administration, Kubernetes software development, or production infrastructure operations. My longer hands-on history is with Azure virtual machines and test environments.

**Software engineering.** I direct AI coding agents to create and revise scripts and components, specify requirements and acceptance tests, review and test results, and debug failures with agent assistance. I developed hundreds of Python and PowerShell scripts this way. I do not independently author production code unaided, and I am not a production software engineer.

**AI systems.** I designed one autonomous documentation pipeline and directed AI agents to build my own extension, agent skills, and Model Context Protocol integrations. I operated and evaluated company-developed agents that I did not design. I do not claim machine-learning engineering, model training, or AI platform ownership.

**Identity and networking.** I contributed documentation covering storage firewalls, private connectivity, access control, and least-privileged roles. I do not claim enterprise identity administration, firewall deployment, web application firewall administration, or network engineering.

**Content management systems.** I have extensive structured authoring experience in XML and in Git-based Markdown publishing, plus basic SharePoint team-site administration. I have not used or administered a headless content management system such as Directus, Contentful, or Strapi.

## What is not published here

This portfolio contains no confidential Microsoft content, internal URLs, proprietary screenshots, private repository content, customer-identifying data, or internal source documents. Where work cannot be shared, I describe the problem and the method at a level that is safe to publish and link only to evidence that is genuinely public.

A note on the public record. Microsoft reassigned documentation bylines during a March 2025 reorganization, so published articles I owned for years no longer carry my name. Git commit history is cryptographically chained and cannot be rewritten the same way, which is why the commit links in this portfolio are the durable proof and the byline is not.
